## 5. The transfer thread — E/P 1-17

The application-first method says to hunt for the recurring exercise that returns to the same
real-world artifact chapter after chapter, then **substitute the learner's own organization's
document**. In [GNP] that thread is **"Examine the CAFR" (1-17)**, and it recurs as the x-16 /
x-17 exercise in later chapters.

We already hold the substitution target: the Palo Duro County ACFR loaded in the W0.2 corpus.
Running 1-17 against the county's own ACFR instead of an arbitrary city converts an academic
exercise into an expert-sequenced walkthrough of the exact document the learner will work on.
This is the highest-leverage move available in the whole build, and it costs nothing.

1-17's own sub-parts map cleanly onto sections already in the corpus: introductory section and
letter of transmittal, audit report, basic financial statements, notes, other supplementary
information, MD&A, and statistical tables.

## 6. Currency check — ⚑ against the 18th edition

| What the text says | Current position | Where it bites |
|---|---|---|
| **"Comprehensive Annual Financial Report (CAFR)"** used throughout, including as a key term and in exercise titles | **GASB 98 renamed it the Annual Comprehensive Financial Report (ACFR).** Content and requirements unchanged | Every occurrence in Ch. 1 and in E/P 1-17. This is the W1 currency patch; the learner must mentally rename while reading |
| 90,056 local governments — 2012 Census of Governments | Superseded by the 2022 Census of Governments | Cosmetic; the magnitude argument is unaffected |
| ~1.6 million NFPs, NCCS file of April 2016 | Dated | Out of scope anyway |
| GASB Codification as of June 30, 2017 | Codification is updated annually | Cite the current edition, never the 2017 one, for any rule a learner would act on |
| Denver CAFR for 2016 used as the running illustration | Dated, and titled CAFR | We substitute the county's own ACFR — see §5 |
| 2016 FASB ASU for NFP reporting (1-13) | Superseded and out of scope | Not assigned |

The chapter's conceptual content — accountability as the cornerstone, interperiod equity, the
fiscal/operational split, the two categories of basic financial statements — is **not**
affected by anything above. Only names, counts, and citation years have moved.

## 7. Coverage gap

The curriculum runs 16 weeks across roughly Chapters 1–12. We hold Chapter 1.

Blocked by the gap, in order of urgency:

1. **Chapter 2** — funds, the reporting model, fund balance. Weeks 1 and 2 both depend on it.
   W1.L4, L5, and L6 and every W1 practice item cite Ch. 2 items (Q 2-5, 2-7, 2-9;
   E/P 2-15, 2-17 → 2-22; Case 2-12). W1.L4 has been built from GASB source material rather
   than the text and is accurate, but its citations are unverified.
2. **Chapter 4** — the dual-track exercises. E/P 4-20 is the worked example in the Coach spec
   and the single most important exercise in the program.
3. **Chapters 3, 5–8** — operating transactions and the governmental fund cycle, Weeks 3–8.
4. **Chapter 11** — single audit, Week 14–15.
5. **Chapter 12** — budgeting and performance measurement.

Chapter 2 is the one to send next; it unlocks the rest of Week 1 and all of Week 2.
