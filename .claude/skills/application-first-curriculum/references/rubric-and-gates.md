# Rubric, Gates, and Coaching Cadence

## The 0–4 competency rubric

Score every competency domain on this scale at each phase boundary. Score on artifacts, never on hours logged or modules completed.

| Level | Descriptor | Evidence standard |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — Unaware** | Doesn't know the task exists | — |
| **1 — Assisted** | Completes with step-by-step direction | Work requires rework before use |
| **2 — Supervised** | Completes from a template; asks good questions | Reviewer finds ≤ 3 substantive comments |
| **3 — Independent** | Completes unaided, on time, correct; recognizes exceptions and escalates appropriately | Reviewer finds ≤ 1 comment; no rework |
| **4 — Improves the process** | Finds errors others missed; improves the template, control, or procedure | Change adopted by the team |

**Comment count is the score signal.** Defining levels by how many substantive review comments an artifact draws makes the rubric resistant to grader generosity — and, when an agent reviews against a deterministic diff, nearly impossible to inflate.

**Set differentiated targets.** Not every competency needs Level 3 by the end. Name which ones do (the daily core) and which can land at 2–3 (the periodic or supporting duties). Level 4 anywhere is a promotion signal.

## Gates

Place a gate at each phase boundary. Gates are hard:

- **Do not advance on a Level 1 in that phase's core competency.** Repeat the phase with different source material — different exercise numbers, different documents, same objectives.
- Advancing someone who can follow a template but cannot tell when the template is wrong is the most common and most expensive training failure. The gate exists to catch exactly that.
- If nobody ever repeats a phase, the gate isn't functioning. Expect and budget for repeats.

**Gate packet** — whoever signs a gate should receive: rubric scores with the specific artifacts cited as evidence, the error-profile trend, the milestone artifacts themselves, any escalation log, and a recommendation with reasoning.

## Error journal

Classify every miss into one of four categories, and track the mix over time rather than a raw error count.

1. **Didn't know the rule**
2. **Knew the rule, applied it at the wrong level or scope**
3. **Mechanical** (arithmetic, transposition, wrong field)
4. **Misread the fact pattern**

Category 2 is usually the headline metric. It's the conceptual failure that follows people into the job, and its decline is the best available proxy for real progress. Category 1 and 2 look identical from the artifact alone and need different remedies, so ask one disambiguating question rather than guessing.

Wire the categories to actions: Category 1 requeues a lesson, Category 2 injects extra reps of that exact distinction, Category 3 is logged only unless the rate spikes (fatigue or bad input design), Category 4 queues fact-extraction practice.

## Portfolio

The learner keeps every graded artifact. It doubles as the annual review packet and as evidence toward a certification application. Make it exportable.

## Coaching cadence (human version)

| Frequency | Activity | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Daily, first phase | Stand-up: what's stuck? | 10 min |
| Weekly | Artifact review against rubric; one worked example demonstrated live | 60 min |
| Weekly | Learner explains one concept back, unaided | 15 min |
| Bi-weekly | Shadow an adjacent function | 2 hrs |
| Monthly | Written rubric scoring; adjust the plan | 45 min |
| Phase end | Gate review; go/no-go | 90 min |

**Model → coach → scaffold → fade.** In the first phase the coach demonstrates *and narrates their own reasoning aloud* — this is the part most supervisors skip and it is the part that transfers. Later the coach watches and prompts, then reviews output only, then acts as an examiner rather than a teacher.

Effort model: roughly 70% live or simulated work, 20% coaching and review, 10% formal reading. Budget 6–8 hours per week inside a normal work week, tapering to ~2 hours of sustainment after the core program.

## When agents replace the coach

The rubric and gates are unchanged. Two adjustments:

- The agent produces the gate packet; a human with authority signs it. Advancing someone through role-linked training is an employment decision and needs an accountable name on it. Four signatures across a sixteen-week program is roughly two hours of human time.
- Where no mentor exists at all, run fully autonomously and mark unsigned gates as **unratified** rather than pretending they're equivalent.
